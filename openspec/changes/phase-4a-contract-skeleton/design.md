## Context

The three published documents under `docs/api/openapi/` are generated by `sky.openapi-conventions` and are not the subject of this change. What is missing is a second, smaller artefact that a CI job can re-derive and diff: a versioned contract bundle under `contracts/sky-api/`.

Two constraints shape the design.

The bundle is produced from a hand-written source, not from the generated service documents. Bundling the service documents would import thirteen hundred lines of generated YAML into a file whose only point is to be diffed, and any change to a service would rewrite the bundle wholesale. The skeleton source carries `info`, `servers` and one path stub, so the bundle is small, legible, and grows one path at a time as the contract is authored.

The lint config mirrors the sibling pattern rather than inventing one. `extends: recommended` is the base, and the skeleton needs only the minimal relaxations to lint clean: `operation-4xx-problem+json` is relaxed because the stub carries no error responses yet, and `operation-operationId` stays on because the stub does declare operation ids.

## Goals

- Produce a bundle that is reproducible: running the same command twice yields the same file.
- Keep the bundle small enough to read in a review and to diff meaningfully.
- Wire a CI job that fails when the committed bundle is not what the current source produces.

## Non-Goals

- Bundling the three service documents under `docs/api/openapi/`. They stay where they are and are regenerated by the build.
- Authoring the full sky API contract. This change scaffolds one path stub; the contract grows by separate changes.
- Replacing `api-contract`'s stated check, which is that regenerating the service documents yields no difference. This change adds a second check on a second artefact, and the two are deliberately separate.

## Decisions

### Source of truth is the hand-written skeleton, not the generated documents

The bundle exists so that drift between the committed bundle and the source can be detected. That only works when the source is something a contributor edits, and the only such thing here is the skeleton. The generated documents are output, not input.

### The bundle is committed, and the CI job diffs against it

The committed `openapi.yaml` is the pinned contract. The CI job re-bundles and diffs. If the two differ, the job fails and the contributor must either update the bundle or fix the source. There is no auto-merge: the bundle is a deliberate artefact and a deliberate commit.

### Lint config mirrors the sibling pattern

`extends: recommended` plus the two relaxations the skeleton needs. Adding rules the skeleton cannot satisfy would be decoration; adding rules nothing exercises would be noise. The config is allowed to relax, and every relaxation is named with the reason.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A bundle that is committed and not regenerated by the build can go stale relative to the source if the CI job is not wired. That is the whole point of the job, and it is wired in the same change.
- The bundle carries one path stub, so the lint config relaxes one rule it would not relax once the contract is complete. That relaxation is named and is removed by the change that adds the real responses.
- The `verify-changelog` gate has no case for `contracts/`, so no changelog bump is demanded. That is the correct reading of the gate and is recorded in proposal.md rather than silently assumed.

## Migration Plan

No migration. The bundle is new, the source is new, and the CI job is additive. Rollback is removing the four new files and the new job.
